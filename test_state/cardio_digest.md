## Electrophysiology & Devices

### [Left Bundle-Branch Area Pacing vs Right Ventricular Pacing for Atrioventricular Block and Preserved Left Ventricular Function: The LEAP-Block Randomized Clinical Trial.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42949742/)
*[JAMA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42949742/) · 2026 Oct 28 · Li X., Wang H., Zhang W. et al.*  
**RCT · relevance 7/10**

**Why it matters:** This is a multicenter RCT in JAMA suggesting LBBAP may reduce HF events and a composite of death, HF, or BiV upgrade versus RVP in patients with preserved LVEF who need high-burden pacing.  
**Bottom line:** LBBAP appears to reduce a composite of death, HF events, or BiV upgrade versus RVP at 24 months, but the benefit is driven mainly by HF events, all-cause death did not differ, and the trial is single-country with an open-label-type design and procedure-dependent success (capture 79.7%). Await replication and guideline review before routinely preferring LBBAP over RVP in this population.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only - remove in product)</summary>

IMPORTANCE: For patients with atrioventricular block requiring high-burden ventricular pacing, the optimal pacing strategy to reduce heart failure (HF) and all-cause mortality remains uncertain. Robust comparative evidence on the clinical outcomes of left bundle-branch area pacing (LBBAP) vs right ventricular pacing (RVP) in this population is needed.
OBJECTIVE: To compare 24-month clinical outcomes of LBBAP vs RVP in patients with atrioventricular block and preserved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF; ≥50%).
DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: The LEAP-Block study was a prospective, multicenter, randomized, controlled, superiority trial conducted across 14 Chinese hospitals from January 2021 to August 2025, with last follow-up on June 3, 2025. Of 551 patients with LVEF 50% or greater requiring ventricular pacing who underwent eligibility assessment, 499 with anticipated ventricular pacing greater than 40% were randomized.
INTERVENTIONS: Patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to LBBAP (n = 249) or RVP (n = 250).
MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: The primary end point was a composite of all-cause death, HF events, or upgrade to biventricular pacing at 24 months. Secondary end points included pacing-induced cardiomyopathy and device-related complications.
RESULTS: The primary analysis included 482 patients (LBBAP, n = 242; RVP, n = 240; mean age, 67 years; 46% female). Acute procedure success was comparable between the 2 groups (LBBAP, 237/242 [97.9%] vs RVP, 239/240 [99.6%]; P = .22), with left bundle-branch capture confirmed in 188 of 236 patients (79.7%). LBBAP significantly reduced the primary end point vs RVP (2.4% vs 12.5%, respectively; absolute risk difference, -10.1% [95% CI, -16.8% to -3.4%]; P = .003). Among secondary outcomes, the LBBAP group had fewer HF events (3.4% vs 11.3%; -7.9% [95% CI, -13.0% to -2.8%]). No significant differences were observed in all-cause death, biventricular pacing upgrade, device-related complications, or pacing-induced cardiomyopathy. The 24-month Kaplan-Meier estimated cumulative incidence of the primary end point was 5.0% for LBBAP vs 13.2% for RVP (hazard ratio, 0.37 [95% CI, 0.19 to 0.73]; P = .004). LBBAP superiority was consistent across all prespecified subgroups.
CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Among patients with atrioventricular block and preserved LVEF requiring high-burden ventricular pacing, LBBAP was superior to RVP in reducing the risk of the composite end point of all-cause death, HF events, or upgrade to biventricular pacing at 24 months.
TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04730921.

DOI: 10.1001/jama.2026.18146 · PMID: 42949742

## Coronary Artery Disease / ACS

### [Performance of 0/1 h versus 0/3 h pathways in patients with suspected myocardial infarction: a systematic review and individual participant data meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42956796/)
*[Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42956796/) · 2026 Oct 29 · Boeddinghaus J., Kunz M., Lopez-Ayala P. et al.*  
**Meta-analysis / Systematic review · relevance 7/10**

**Why it matters:** This individual participant data meta-analysis of five RCTs tests whether the guideline-endorsed 0/1 h troponin pathway for suspected myocardial infarction improves emergency department efficiency without compromising safety, which is central to how EDs implement rapid rule-out.  
**Bottom line:** The 0/1 h pathway did not significantly shorten emergency department length of stay or clearly increase direct discharge, and its 30-day safety estimate (OR 0.83, 95% CI 0.45-1.53) is too imprecise to show it is safe or unsafe. Implementation should weigh local diagnostic and organisational factors, since heterogeneity was high and the safety outcome was redefined after the individual participant data were received.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only - remove in product)</summary>

BACKGROUND: In patients with suspected myocardial infarction, the 0/1 h pathway (blood draws at 0 h and 1 h) is recommended in current guidelines for early risk stratification. However, individual trials used different safety outcomes, and efficacy outcomes were inconclusive or not assessed. We conducted a systematic review and individual participant data meta-analysis of all randomised controlled trials to synthesise the totality of the evidence, assess heterogeneity across trials, and evaluate clinically relevant outcomes using harmonised definitions across trials.
METHODS: In this systematic review and individual participant data meta-analysis, we searched PubMed, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Scopus, and the International Clinical Trials Registry Platform from inception to July 6, 2026, without language restrictions, for randomised trials comparing the safety and efficacy of 0/1 h versus 0/3 h pathways (blood draws at 0 h and 3 h, ie, standard care). Individual participant data were obtained and outcome definitions were harmonised. The primary analysis used a two-stage random-effects meta-analysis. After receipt of the individual participant data but before data harmonisation or analysis, the primary safety outcome was refined to a composite of all-cause death or new type 1 myocardial infarction within 30 days, from the originally specified composite of all-cause death or any new myocardial infarction. The primary efficacy outcomes were emergency department length of stay and direct discharge from the emergency department. This meta-analysis is registered with PROSPERO (CRD420261383452).
FINDINGS: Our systematic review identified five randomised trials, all of which provided individual participant data. Among these, the definition of the primary safety outcome varied, and in four trials emergency department length of stay was not reported for the whole analysis population. We included 110 933 patient presentations (55 489 managed with the 0/1 h pathway and 55 444 managed with the 0/3 h pathway). Within 30 days, all-cause death or new type 1 myocardial infarction occurred in 446 patient presentations (0·8%) with the 0/1 h pathway versus 562 patient presentations (1·0%) with the 0/3 h pathway (random-effects pooled odds ratio [OR] 0·83 [95% CI 0·45-1·53]; I2=62·9%; τ2=0·143). Emergency department length of stay was 314 min (IQR 211-481) in the 0/1 h pathway group versus 308 min (209-453) in the 0/3 h pathway group (random-effects pooled geometric mean ratio 1·03 [95% CI 0·85-1·25]; I2=99·2%; τ2=0·024). Direct discharge from the emergency department occurred in 37 190 patient presentations (67·0%) with the 0/1 h pathway versus 36 485 patient presentations (65·8%) with the 0/3 h pathway (random-effects pooled OR 1·31 [95% CI 0·79-2·16]; I2=97·8%; τ2=0·156).
INTERPRETATION: Across the available randomised trials, short-term adverse event rates were low with both pathways, but the pooled safety estimate remained imprecise. On average, earlier cardiac troponin retesting did not reduce emergency department length of stay or clearly increase direct discharge, and substantial between-study heterogeneity indicates that treatment effects might not translate uniformly across health-care systems. For hospitals considering transitioning from a 0/3 h to a 0/1 h pathway, implementation decisions should take broader diagnostic and organisational considerations into account.
FUNDING: Swiss National Science Foundation, Swiss Heart Foundation, University Hospital Basel, University of Basel, and Foundation for Cardiovascular Research Basel.

DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01767-8 · PMID: 42956796

### [Clopidogrel Versus Aspirin Monotherapy According to Atherothrombotic Risk Profile.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42953942/)
*[Circ Cardiovasc Interv](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42953942/) · 2026 Oct 29 · Lee J., Gim D., Park Y. et al.*  
**RCT · relevance 6/10**
*also relevant for: Interventional Cardiology*

**Why it matters:** It suggests that clopidogrel monotherapy may be preferable to aspirin after DAPT in higher atherothrombotic-risk post-PCI patients, but this is a post hoc subgroup analysis with a non-significant interaction test.  
**Bottom line:** In this post hoc analysis of SMART-CHOICE 3, clopidogrel monotherapy reduced MACCE versus aspirin in higher-risk patients (TIMI-SP ≥3 or complex PCI) after DAPT without more bleeding, and showed no benefit in lower-risk patients. The interaction P=0.242 means this is hypothesis-generating and should be confirmed before changing routine practice.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only - remove in product)</summary>

BACKGROUND: Clopidogrel monotherapy has shown superior efficacy compared with aspirin after completion of dual antiplatelet therapy following percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). However, whether treatment effects differ according to overall atherothrombotic risk integrating clinical and angiographic factors remains unclear.
METHODS: This post hoc analysis of the SMART-CHOICE 3 (Smart Angioplasty Research Team: Choice of Optimal Anti-Thrombotic Strategy in Patients Undergoing Implantation of Coronary Drug-Eluting Stents 3) randomized trial included patients who completed guideline-directed dual antiplatelet therapy after PCI. Atherothrombotic risk was defined using the a Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction Risk Score for Secondary Prevention and complex PCI. Patients with Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction Risk Score for Secondary Prevention ≥3 or complex PCI were classified as higher risk, and those with a Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction Risk Score for Secondary Prevention <3 without complex PCI were classified as lower risk. The primary end point was major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (death, myocardial infarction, or stroke). Bleeding was defined as Bleeding Academic Research Consortium type 2, 3, or 5.
RESULTS: Among 5506 patients, 4486 (81.5%) were at higher risk and 1020 (18.5%) were at lower risk. Over a median 2.3 years, the incidence of major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events was higher in the higher-risk group (6.1% versus 3.1%; hazard ratio, 1.83 [95% CI, 1.17-2.78]; P=0.007). In higher-risk patients, clopidogrel reduced major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events compared with aspirin (4.6% versus 7.4%; hazard ratio, 0.67 [95% CI, 0.50-0.89]; P=0.006), with no difference in lower-risk patients (3.0% versus 3.2%; hazard ratio, 1.03; P=0.945; interaction P=0.242). Bleeding was similar between groups.
CONCLUSIONS: After completion of dual antiplatelet therapy following PCI, clopidogrel monotherapy was associated with lower ischemic risk than aspirin in patients with higher atherothrombotic risk, without increased bleeding.
REGISTRATION: URL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT04418479.

DOI: 10.1161/CIRCINTERVENTIONS.126.017277 · PMID: 42953942
</details>
