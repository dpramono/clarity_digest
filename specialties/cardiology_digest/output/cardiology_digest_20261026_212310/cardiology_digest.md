### [ICare-FASTER (Improving Care by Faster Risk Stratification Through Use of High-Sensitivity Point-of-Care Troponin in Patients Presenting With Possible Acute Coronary Syndrome in the Emergency Department): A Stepped-Wedge Cluster-Randomized Trial.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42895015/)
*[Circulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42895015/) · 2026 Oct 19 · Than M., Pickering J., Joyce L. et al.*  
**RCT · relevance 8/10  ⭐ practice-changing  · interventional**

**Why it matters:** This large cluster-randomized trial demonstrates that point-of-care high-sensitivity troponin I safely accelerates emergency department throughput without increasing adverse cardiac events.  
**Bottom line:** Implementing point-of-care hs-cTnI into emergency department accelerated diagnostic pathways reduced length of stay by 47 minutes while maintaining equivalent 30-day safety compared to central laboratory testing. This provides strong support for transitioning from lab-based to point-of-care hs-cTn to alleviate ED crowding.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only)</summary>

BACKGROUND: Point-of-care high-sensitivity cTn (cardiac troponin; hs-cTn) testing has the potential to expedite decision-making and reduce emergency department (ED) length of stay for patients presenting with possible myocardial infarction by ensuring that results are consistently available when sought by clinicians. We assessed the real-life effectiveness and safety of implementing point-of-care hs-cTn testing in the ED.
METHODS: We conducted a pragmatic, stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trial. The control arm was usual care with an accelerated diagnostic pathway using a single-sample rule-out step with a central laboratory hs-cTn assay. The intervention arm used the same pathway with a point-of-care hs-cTnI assay. The primary effectiveness outcome was ED length of stay assessed using a generalized linear mixed model. The safety outcome was myocardial infarction or cardiac death within 30 days.
RESULTS: Six sites participated, with 59 980 ED presentations (44 747 individuals; mean 61±19 years of age; 49.5% female) from February 2023 through January 2025; 31 392 presentations were during the intervention arm. After adjustment for covariates associated with length of stay, the intervention reduced length of stay by 13% (95% CI, 9%-16%; P<0.001), corresponding to a reduction of 47 minutes (95% CI, 33-61 minutes) from a mean length of stay in the control arm of 376 minutes. The 30-day myocardial infarction or cardiac death rate was similar in the control and intervention arms (0.39% and 0.39%, respectively; P=0.54).
CONCLUSIONS: Implementation of whole-blood hs-cTnI testing at the point of care into an accelerated diagnostic pathway was safe and reduced length of stay in the ED compared with laboratory testing.

### [Immediate Complete Revascularization During Index Admission in Older Patients With ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42925297/)
*[JACC Cardiovasc Interv](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42925297/) · 2026 Oct 22 · Cho K., Kim M., Ahn J. et al.*  
**RCT · relevance 7/10  · interventional**

**Why it matters:** In older STEMI patients with multivessel disease, immediate complete revascularization during index primary PCI was associated with increased 1-year adverse events compared to in-hospital staged revascularization.  
**Bottom line:** A staged approach to non-culprit lesions may be safer than immediate complete revascularization during primary PCI in patients aged 70 and older. While hypothesis-generating, these data advise caution against prolonged single-setting revascularization in elderly STEMI patients.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only)</summary>

BACKGROUND: Whether age affects the comparative treatment effect of immediate complete revascularization (CR) vs staged CR during the index admission in patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction and multivessel disease remains unknown.
OBJECTIVES: This study aimed to assess how age influences clinical outcomes in patients with with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction and multivessel disease randomized to immediate or in-hospital staged CR.
METHODS: This is a secondary analysis of the OPTION-STEMI (Optimal Timing of Fractional Flow Reserve-Guided Complete Revascularization for Non-Infarct-Related Artery in ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction with Multivessel Disease) trial, which included 994 patients randomized to immediate or in-hospital staged CR. The primary endpoint at 1 year, defined as a composite of all-cause death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or any unplanned revascularization, was analyzed according to age and CR strategy.
RESULTS: A significant interaction was found between age, as a continuous variable, and CR strategy for the primary endpoint (interaction P = 0.015). The HR for the primary endpoint was initially higher in the staged CR arm until approximately 67 years, after which it increased steadily in the immediate CR arm. In the binary analysis, a significantly higher incidence of the primary endpoint was observed in the immediate CR arm compared with the staged CR arm in patients ≥70 years (45 of 202 [22.3%] vs 29 of 210 [13.8%]; HR: 1.68; 95% CI: 1.06-2.69; log-rank P = 0.029), but not in patients <70 years (20 of 296 [6.8%] vs 24 of 286 [8.4%]; HR: 0.80; 95% CI: 0.45-1.45; log-rank P = 0.470) (multiplicative interaction P = 0.054; relative excess risk due to interaction P = 0.016).
CONCLUSIONS: This post hoc analysis of the OPTION-STEMI trial demonstrates that older patients experience significantly worse clinical outcomes with immediate CR than with in-hospital staged CR. These findings suggest that age modifies the treatment effect of the CR strategy and warrant confirmation in adequately powered trials (Timing of FFR-guided PCI for Non-IRA in STEMI and MVD [OPTION-STEMI]; NCT04626882).

### [Coronary artery disease management in patients with severe aortic stenosis undergoing transcatheter aortic valve implantation: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42910974/)
*[EuroIntervention](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42910974/) · 2026 Oct 19 · Bujak K., Giacoppo D., Laudani C. et al.*  
**Meta-analysis / Systematic review · relevance 7/10  · interventional**

**Why it matters:** Synthesizes recent RCT evidence on revascularization prior to TAVI, supporting physiology-guided PCI as the optimal strategy to reduce ischemic events despite increased bleeding risks.  
**Bottom line:** In patients undergoing TAVI with concomitant coronary artery disease, routine revascularization reduces MACE and unplanned interventions compared to conservative management, with physiology-guided PCI showing the most favorable risk-benefit balance.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only)</summary>

BACKGROUND: The optimal management of coronary artery disease in patients with severe aortic stenosis undergoing transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) remains uncertain.
AIMS: We aimed to compare clinical outcomes between routine coronary revascularisation and conservative management in patients undergoing TAVI and to assess the relative effectiveness of individual revascularisation strategies by performing a meta-analysis of randomised trials.
METHODS: We conducted a pairwise meta-analysis comparing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with conservative treatment and a network meta-analysis comparing the relative effectiveness of individual strategies, including angiography-guided and physiology-guided PCI. The primary outcome was a study-defined composite endpoint of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). Secondary endpoints included the individual outcomes of all-cause death, cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, stroke, unplanned revascularisation, and major bleeding.
RESULTS: Five trials (N=1,644 patients) were included in the systematic review, and four were included in the primary quantitative synthesis (N=1,476). Compared with conservative treatment, routine PCI was associated with lower risks of MACE (risk ratio [RR] 0.80, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.65-0.98) and unplanned revascularisation (RR 0.30, 95% CI: 0.11-0.83) and an increased risk of major bleeding (RR 1.57, 95% CI: 1.07-2.32). In the network meta-analysis, physiology-guided PCI significantly reduced MACE, stroke, and unplanned revascularisation compared with conservative treatment and ranked highest for all ischaemic outcomes among revascularisation strategies.
CONCLUSIONS: Among patients referred for TAVI, routine revascularisation reduces the risk of ischaemic outcomes compared with conservative treatment. In this setting, physiology-guided PCI may be associated with the most favourable efficacy profile across available strategies. (PROSPERO: CRD420261330194).

### [Sirolimus-Coated Balloon Angioplasty for Femoropopliteal Artery Disease Without Tissue Loss: A Secondary Analysis of the SirPAD Trial.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42909452/)
*[J Am Coll Cardiol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42909452/) · 2026 Oct 05 · Tsaftaridis N., Wolf S., Engelberger R. et al.*  
**RCT · relevance 7/10  · interventional**

**Why it matters:** Provides randomized evidence supporting sirolimus-coated balloons as an effective non-paclitaxel alternative for reducing target-lesion revascularization in femoropopliteal peripheral artery disease.  
**Bottom line:** In patients with femoropopliteal disease without tissue loss, sirolimus-coated balloon angioplasty reduced 1-year target-lesion revascularization and major adverse limb events compared with plain balloon angioplasty. While descriptive, these findings strengthen the clinical utility of 'limus'-based drug delivery in peripheral endovascular interventions.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only)</summary>

BACKGROUND: Whether sirolimus-coated balloon angioplasty performs better than uncoated balloon angioplasty among patients with exertional symptoms due to femoropopliteal artery disease remains uncertain.
OBJECTIVES: This study sought to describe 1-year outcomes after sirolimus-coated balloon angioplasty among participants in the SirPAD (Sirolimus-Coated Balloon for Peripheral Artery Disease) trial with femoropopliteal artery disease.
METHODS: In this analysis of the multicenter, randomized, blinded-endpoint SirPAD trial, we evaluated outcomes for patients with femoropopliteal target lesions and without tissue loss (Rutherford category ≤4, Fontaine stage ≤III). A secondary subgroup was restricted to patients with a lower disease burden, excluding ischemic rest pain, prior major amputation, or below-the-knee revascularization. We compared the 1-year rate of: 1) target-lesion revascularization (TLR); 2) the composite of TLR and any unplanned amputation; 3) major adverse limb events; and 4) all-cause death.
RESULTS: Among 597 patients, median age was 71 years, 34.7% were women, and median target-lesion length was 150 mm; 53.8% had total occlusion. After 1 year, 50 patients (17.0%) had undergone TLR in the sirolimus-coated balloon group vs 82 (27.1%) among control subjects (absolute risk difference [ARD]: -10.1%; 95% CI: -16.6 to -3.0). Any amputation or any TLR occurred in 17.7% and 27.1% of patients respectively (ARD: -9.4%; 95% CI: -16.0 to -2.7). Major adverse limb events occurred in 4.1% vs 8.9% of patients, respectively (ARD: -4.8%; 95% CI: -9.0 to -0.8). One-year all-cause death was similar (3.7% vs 5.3%).
CONCLUSIONS: Among patients with femoropopliteal artery disease and no tissue loss, sirolimus-coated balloon angioplasty was associated with a lower 1-year rate of TLR compared with uncoated balloon angioplasty. These findings are descriptive rather than confirmatory. (Sirolimus-Coated Balloon for Peripheral Artery Disease; NCT04238546).

### [Three ablation strategies using very high-power short-duration catheter in paroxysmal atrial fibrillation: The Q-INDEX randomized trial.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42909425/)
*[Heart Rhythm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42909425/) · 2026 Oct 23 · Lee S., Cho Y., Han S. et al.*  
**RCT · relevance 7/10  · interventional**

**Why it matters:** This randomized trial cautions against uniform very high-power short-duration (vHPSD) ablation across all left atrial segments due to lower 1-year durability compared to standard AI-guided or hybrid strategies.  
**Bottom line:** While uniform vHPSD (90 W/4 s) significantly cuts procedural ablation time, it yielded lower 1-year freedom from atrial arrhythmias (77.6% vs 92.0%) compared to AI guidance. A hybrid approach—using AI-guided lesions anteriorly and vHPSD elsewhere—balances procedural efficiency with clinical efficacy.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only)</summary>

BACKGROUND: Very high-power short-duration (vHPSD; 90 W/4 sec) ablation shortens pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) but produces shallower lesions that may be inadequate in the thick anterior left atrium.
OBJECTIVE: To compare vHPSD, ablation index (AI)-guided, and hybrid strategies for PVI in paroxysmal atrial fibrillation.
METHODS: In this multicenter, randomized, open-label trial, 150 patients with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation were assigned 1:1:1 with the QDOT MICRO catheter to (1) AI-guided (AI 500 anterior, 400 elsewhere), (2) hybrid (AI-guided anterior [AI 500], vHPSD elsewhere), or (3) vHPSD (90 W/4 sec, all segments). The primary endpoint was total ablation time; secondary endpoints included acute PV reconnection, safety, and 1-year freedom from atrial arrhythmia.
RESULTS: Acute PVI was achieved in all patients. Total ablation time was longest with AI-guided and shortest with vHPSD ablation (median 2220 vs 1450 vs 794 sec; P < .001). Acute reconnection across the 14 PV segments was lowest with AI-guided and highest with vHPSD (7.7% vs 9.4% vs 10.6%; P = .40). One-year freedom from atrial arrhythmia was 92.0%, 85.4%, and 77.6%, respectively (log-rank P = .13; for AI-guided vs vHPSD; P = .049). No tamponade occurred; steam pops occurred only in the 90 W groups. Two minor periprocedural strokes occurred (both vHPSD; full recovery), and silent cerebral lesions on magnetic resonance imaging did not differ among the groups (5%, 10%, 0%; P = .35).
CONCLUSION: Uniform vHPSD more than halved PVI ablation time but was associated with the lowest 1-year efficacy, whereas the hybrid strategy preserved efficiency without significant loss of durability. These exploratory findings-from a trial powered for ablation time-support region-tailored lesion depth in point-by-point ablation and warrant confirmation.

DOI: 10.1016/j.hrthm.2026.09.038 · PMID: 42909425
</details>

### [Comparison of Drug-Coated Versus Conventional Balloons for the Side Branches of Bifurcation Lesions: OCVC-BIF Study.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42900708/)
*[Circ Cardiovasc Interv](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42900708/) · 2026 Oct · Ishihara T., Dohi T., Sakamoto D. et al.*  
**RCT · relevance 7/10  · interventional**

**Why it matters:** Managing side branches in coronary bifurcation PCI remains technically difficult, and this randomized trial provides clear evidence that drug-coated balloons reduce angiographic side-branch restenosis.  
**Bottom line:** In patients undergoing provisional bifurcation stenting with kissing balloon inflation, additional side-branch dilation with a drug-coated balloon significantly reduced 9-month side-branch restenosis (8.1% vs 18.3%) compared with conventional balloon angioplasty, without a significant difference in 1-year MACE.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only)</summary>

BACKGROUND: Coronary bifurcation lesions remain one of the most challenging lesions. In particular, satisfactory clinical outcomes have not yet been achieved for side branches. The use of drug-coated balloons (DCBs) has demonstrated acceptable clinical outcomes in native coronary arteries. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of additional DCB inflation in the side branches of bifurcation lesions.
METHODS: The OCVC-BIF study (Comparison of Drug-Coated Versus Conventional Balloons for the Side Branch of Bifurcation Lesions) was conducted as a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial. Patients with bifurcation lesions scheduled for drug-eluting stent (DES) implantation in the main vessel followed by kissing balloon inflation were enrolled in the study. The patients were randomized to receive either additional dilatation of the side branch using a DCB after DES implantation with kissing balloon inflation (DCB group) or only DES implantation with kissing balloon inflation using conventional balloons (non-DCB group). The primary end point was restenosis of the side branch, assessed using scheduled coronary angiography at 9 months or symptom-driven coronary angiography during the 1-year follow-up. Restenosis was defined as ≥50% stenosis of the side branch, determined using 3-dimensional bifurcation quantitative coronary angiography. The key secondary end points included major adverse cardiac events at 12 months, which were defined as a composite of cardiac death, myocardial infarction, clinically driven target lesion revascularization, and stent thrombosis.
RESULTS: In total, 299 patients were randomized into the DCB (151 patients) and non-DCB (148 patients) groups between October 2019 and September 2024. Serial 3-dimensional bifurcation quantitative coronary angiography evaluation was performed in 262 (87.6%) patients. The restenosis rate was lower in the DCB group than that in the non-DCB group (8.1% versus 18.3%, P=0.012). However, the incidence of major adverse cardiac events at 12 months was similar between them (10.1% versus 12.4%, P=0.58).
CONCLUSIONS: Additional DCB inflation in the side branch after main-vessel DES implantation with kissing balloon inflation decreases restenosis in the side branch in the treatment of bifurcation lesions.

### [Individualized Cardiac Index-Guided Hemodynamic Therapy in High-Risk Patients Undergoing Elective Major Abdominal Surgery: The PELICAN Randomized Clinical Trial.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42896534/)
*[JAMA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42896534/) · 2026 Oct 19 · Saugel B., Meidert A., Meyhoff C. et al.*  
**RCT · relevance 7/10  · interventional**

**Why it matters:** This large multicenter RCT in JAMA demonstrates that intraoperative hemodynamic therapy tailored to baseline cardiac index does not prevent postoperative cardiovascular, renal, or infectious complications compared to routine care.  
**Bottom line:** In high-risk patients undergoing elective major abdominal surgery, individualized cardiac index-guided hemodynamic therapy did not reduce 7-day adverse outcomes. Routine hemodynamic management remains the standard of care.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only)</summary>

IMPORTANCE: It remains unclear if intraoperative hemodynamic therapy should be guided by cardiac index.
OBJECTIVE: To evaluate whether individualized cardiac index-guided hemodynamic therapy vs routine hemodynamic therapy improves outcomes in high-risk patients undergoing elective major abdominal surgery.
DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This multicenter, single-blind, superiority randomized clinical trial enrolled high-risk patients undergoing elective major abdominal surgery between October 8, 2023, and October 25, 2025, at 12 university medical centers in Austria, the Czech Republic, Denmark, Germany, and Spain. The date of last follow-up was January 15, 2026.
INTERVENTIONS: Patients were randomized to individualized cardiac index-guided hemodynamic therapy targeting the patient's preoperative resting cardiac index vs routine hemodynamic therapy (with blinded cardiac index monitoring).
MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: The primary outcome was a composite of acute kidney injury, acute myocardial injury, severe infectious complications, nonfatal cardiac arrest, or death within the first 7 postoperative days. Secondary outcomes included the individual components of the primary outcome within the first 7 postoperative days.
RESULTS: Of the 1128 patients enrolled, 1111 were randomized and 1095 were included in the primary analysis (median age, 67 years [IQR, 59-74 years]; 49.4% were female). The primary outcome occurred in 243 of 549 patients (44.3%) in the individualized cardiac index-guided hemodynamic therapy group vs 256 of 546 patients (46.9%) in the routine hemodynamic therapy group (relative risk, 0.94 [95.1% CI, 0.83-1.08]; P = .38). There were no between-group differences for the incidence of the individual components of the primary outcome within the first 7 postoperative days.
CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Among high-risk patients undergoing elective major abdominal surgery, individualized cardiac index-guided hemodynamic therapy targeting the patient's preoperative resting cardiac index did not reduce the incidence of a composite outcome of acute kidney injury, acute myocardial injury, severe infectious complications, nonfatal cardiac arrest, or death within the first 7 postoperative days compared with routine hemodynamic therapy.
TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05648279.

DOI: 10.1001/jama.2026.18574 · PMID: 42896534
</details>

## Heart Failure / Cardiomyopathies

### [A Common CD36 Variant and the Genetic Landscape of Dilated Cardiomyopathy in Individuals of African Ancestry.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42895716/)
*[JAMA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42895716/) · 2026 Oct 19 · Challa S., Biddinger K., Abramowitz S. et al.*  
**Large observational / Registry · relevance 7/10**

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates that a common CD36 nonsense variant accounts for a larger population attributable risk of dilated cardiomyopathy in individuals of African ancestry than TTN truncating variants, underscoring the need to update genetic testing panels.  
**Bottom line:** The CD36 Y325* variant confers a significant risk of dilated cardiomyopathy in individuals of African ancestry (OR 4.8 for homozygotes) with a 9.0% population attributable fraction, surpassing TTN. Clinicians and geneticists should ensure CD36 is evaluated when performing cardiomyopathy genetic testing in this population.

<details><summary>Original abstract (internal QC only)</summary>

IMPORTANCE: Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is a major cause of heart failure that disproportionately affects individuals of predominantly African ancestry, among whom familial clustering of disease is more pronounced relative to those of predominantly European ancestry. Truncating variants in TTN, which encodes the sarcomeric protein titin, are the most common established monogenic cause of DCM. A common CD36 nonsense variant enriched among individuals of African ancestry has also been shown to account for a substantial burden of DCM.
OBJECTIVE: To compare the contribution of a CD36 nonsense variant to DCM risk with that of truncating variants in TTN and pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in other established genes for DCM.
DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: Multicohort genetic association study including participants of African and European ancestry with exome or genome sequence data and DCM case status from the following 4 US datasets: All of Us Research Program (recruitment: 2017-present; last follow-up: August 27, 2024), Million Veteran Program (recruitment: 2011-present; last follow-up: September 30, 2024), Penn Medicine Biobank (recruitment: 2013-present; last follow-up: November 14, 2022), and the DCM Precision Medicine Study (recruitment: 2016-2020; last follow-up: March 15, 2020).
EXPOSURE: Carrier status for TTN truncating variants, pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in 11 high-confidence genes for DCM, and the nonsense variant CD36 Y325* (0, 1, or 2 copies).
MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: Risk of DCM, prevalence of variant carriers among DCM cases, and population attributable fraction of DCM.
RESULTS: Among 82 623 individuals of predominantly African ancestry across 4 cohorts, the mean age was 53.4 years (SD, 13.9 years), 47 124 were male, and 1316 had DCM. Individuals who were homozygous for the CD36 Y325* risk allele had a higher risk of DCM (odds ratio [OR], 4.8; 95% CI, 3.1-7.3) as did those who were heterozygous (OR, 1.4; 95% CI, 1.2-1.7). TTN truncating variants also conferred an elevated risk of DCM in participants of African ancestry (OR, 8.5; 95% CI, 5.5-13.0). Among 1316 individuals of African ancestry classified as DCM cases, 33 (2.5%) were homozygous for CD36, 56 (4.3%) had TTN truncating variants, and 16 (1.2%) had pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in other high-confidence genes for DCM. In population-level analyses incorporating both heterozygous and homozygous CD36 Y325* carriers, the population attributable fraction for CD36 (9.0% [6.2% for heterozygous carriers and 2.8% for homozygous carriers]) surpassed the population attributable fraction for TTN truncating variants (3.7%).
