### Five-Year Follow-Up of Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement With Self-Expanding Intra-Annular Versus Commercially Available Valves.

### Prevalence and Prognostic Significance of Exercise-Accentuated J-Point Elevation in Brugada Syndrome.
- **Journal**: JACC Clin Electrophysiol (2026)
- **Score**: 6/10 | **Subgroup**: Electrophysiology & Devices
- **Bottom Line**: Exercise-accentuated precordial J-point elevation (≥1 mm in V1/V2) occurs in nearly 30% of Brugada syndrome patients and strongly correlates with exertion-triggered cardiac events. While not predictive of overall events, exercise stress testing may aid in personalized exercise counseling and exertional risk stratification.

### Strength-Based Exercise Is Not Associated With Ventricular Arrhythmia in Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy-Towards Safer Exercise Options.

- **Score**: 7/10 | **Subgroup**: Heart Failure / Cardiomyopathies
- **Bottom Line**: In patients with or at risk for arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy, strength-based exercise was not associated with an increased risk of sustained ventricular arrhythmias compared with sedentary status, in contrast to high-risk endurance training. These registry findings support more nuanced, shared decision-making regarding resistance training in ACM management.

### Phenotype-specific prognostic implications of pulse pressure in heart failure.
- **Journal**: Eur J Heart Fail (2026)
- **Score**: 6/10 | **Subgroup**: Heart Failure / Cardiomyopathies
- **Bottom Line**: In this pooled analysis of over 60,000 trial patients, low pulse pressure was associated with worse outcomes in HFrEF (reflecting poor stroke volume), whereas high pulse pressure signaled higher risk in HFpEF and HFmrEF (reflecting arterial stiffness), though incremental prognostic discrimination beyond SBP is modest.
- **URL**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42725585/

### Impact of Initial Valve Type on Reintervention for TAVR Failure: From the EXPLANTORREDO-TAVR International Registry.

- **Score**: 6/10 | **Subgroup**: Structural Heart Disease
- **Bottom Line**: Although failed balloon-expandable valves fail later and more frequently from structural deterioration compared to self-expanding valves (which fail earlier with more paravalvular leak), initial valve platform does not impact short- or long-term survival following redo-TAVR or surgical explant.
